Snow on Teton Pass

Travelers relying on Teton Pass can now expect anything from nice conditions, to slick, icy roads.

This past Sunday, snow finally struck Teton Pass causing many tourists to sidetrack their trips.

Entering the Park

One of the main ways of entering Grand Teton National Park is through the Teton Pass. However, the pass has recently been covered in snow, causing many tourists to find alternative routes, or skip the park altogether.

At its highest elevation, Teton Pass reaches 8,432 feet above sea level and is one of the first roads on the way to Grand Teton National Park to receive snow.

As the most common route to get to the National Park, many tourists will begin avoiding the park. Though the pass is still open, during harsher conditions, it is apt to close.

Luckily, there are other ways of entering the park. Many visitors can enter the park by going through Yellowstone. Though this can be more expensive, it is the safest way when the pass is snow ridden.

Currently, the pass is in fair condition, but those conditions can change quickly. You can find more updates on the pass and see the road conditions by visiting WYDOT 511 Service.

Even when travel can be hard to time correctly for Grand Teton National Park, it is always worth the view.
